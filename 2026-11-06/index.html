Pied-à-terre Ruling, Chrysler Dates Matter, Office Leasing 54% | NYC CRE News Nov 6, 2026

New York City's pied-à-terre surcharge rollout challenge is dismissed; Manhattan office leasing jumps 54%; Genesis closes $669 million for Rangel Houses; and Friday's closing Dates Matter story connects Lincoln's 1860 election to Cooper Union and the Chrysler Building's $235 million repositioning.

Eric Michael Anton · 1:14 · MP3

Transcript

It's Friday, November 6th. Welcome to the Anton Group News of the Day, I'm Eric Michael Anton.

The 10-year Treasury closed Thursday at 4.84%. SOFR is at 3.94%.

A state appeals court dismissed the challenge to New York City's pied-à-terre surcharge rollout for lack of standing. The single-family threshold is $5 million; co-ops and condos have a separate threshold. Constitutional suits remain pending.

Manhattan office leasing jumped 54% in October. Asking rents matched their pre-pandemic average, availability tightened, and sublet supply fell to its lowest level in years. Landlords are competing for expanding tenants in a strengthening market.

Genesis closed $669 million to rehabilitate Harlem's Rangel Houses. The financing will modernize the public housing campus, including apartments and community facilities, bringing major investment into the city's affordable housing stock.

Dates Matter. November 6, 1860: Abraham Lincoln won the presidency after his Cooper Union address earlier that year. Cooper Union still owns the land beneath the Chrysler Building. Tishman Speyer signed a 150-year ground lease, with $235 million to reposition the office tower.

That's New York. Have a great weekend.
